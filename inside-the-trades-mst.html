How MST trades

Looks forBuys only, on the Dow (YM) 10 minute chart. It waits for a dip or a pause in an uptrend to run out of steam, then buys at the close of the candle where its proprietary confluences confirm the uptrend is resuming.
When it tradesFirst entries from 11:30 AM to 2:55 PM Eastern on Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday (Low Risk V1 skips Tuesday). After a stop out it can buy again at about the same price, and these second entries can come after 2:55 PM (for example 3:34 PM in the backtest). Trades still open late in the day are normally closed at 4:30 PM Eastern. It trades less than once a day on average (about 0.43 trades a day).
Stop and targetsStop 65 points below entry, target 85 points above. A trailing stop is also on: once a trade is up 70 points, the stop moves up to about the entry price and then follows price 70 points behind.
Contracts and whyAlways 1 full size Dow (YM) contract, so the planned risk is the same on every trade: about $325 to the stop, about $425 to the target. Results include trading fees, so a full loss shows as -$329.36 and a full win as +$420.64.

Quick terms reminder

BacktestA test of an algo's rules on past market data. These examples come from tick data backtests in the NinjaTrader Strategy Analyzer, not live trading, and results include trading fees.
Long or shortLong means buying to profit if price rises. Short means selling first to profit if price falls.
ContractOne unit of a futures market. These algos trade the Micro Nasdaq (MNQ), the Micro Russell (M2K) and the full size Dow (YM).
Point and tickA point (pts) is one full unit of price movement. A tick is the smallest step price can move.
SetupThe market pattern an algo waits for before it is allowed to enter.
EntryThe moment and price where the algo opens the trade.
StopThe price where a losing trade is closed automatically. It is set before the trade is entered, so the loss is planned to be about a set size. Fees, and fills a little past the stop price, can make it a little larger.
Target (PT)A price where profit is taken. With more than one contract, part of the position exits at each target (PT1, PT2, PT3).
DEC26The December 2026 futures contract, the one these backtests traded.
Low Risk V1The Low Risk version 1 settings of each algo, the settings used for these backtests.
Trailing stopA stop that follows a winning trade, so it locks in profit if price turns back.
Edge LeverageA setting that makes the next trade after a stop out that day use more contracts. It is on by default in the version 1 (V1) templates of OGX and RBO, at every risk level (Low, Medium and High), for both cash and prop firm accounts. In every other version and every other algo it is off by default or is not a setting at all. In these backtests OGX Low Risk V1 had it on, so its next trade after a stop out that day used 4 contracts instead of 2.
CandleOne bar on the chart that covers a set amount of time, like 5 minutes. The thick body shows where price opened and closed in that time, and the thin lines above and below show the highest and lowest prices it reached.
Candle close vs touchOGX enters the moment price moves through its range level. URGO, B2X, FSA and MST wait for a candle to close before they enter.
Reading the chartsGreen candles closed higher than they opened, red candles closed lower. The blue arrow is the entry, a black X is an exit, the red dashed line is the stop and the green dashed lines are targets. An amber shaded box marks the setup on most charts. Times are Eastern, and each candle is labeled by the time it closed, the same as NinjaTrader.
